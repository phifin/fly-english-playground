## Answer rationale and ambiguity review

### A1 — The Lantern Harbor Map

1. **Choice — `boathouse`.** The harbor note explicitly places the boathouse opposite the blue gate. The pier is next to the gate, and the lighthouse is behind the boathouse. No other option fits “opposite.”
2. **Gap — `is`.** The constrained instruction asks for the present form of *be*, and the sentence names one lighthouse. `Are` does not agree with that singular subject. No contracted alternative fits the blank.
3. **Match — red lantern → above the gate; bell → inside the lighthouse; three blue boats → beside the pier.** Each object and place appears as a distinct statement in the note, and each right-hand location is used once. No competing pair is supported.
4. **Order — gate, boathouse, lighthouse.** The route note marks the steps first, next and then. The prompt repeats that ordering scheme, so there is no alternative valid permutation.
5. **Writing — reflective only.** The model is an original example using the harbor clues. Learners review agreement and whether they gave a correct location; the engine must not treat this as an objectively correct/incorrect response.

### B2 — The Clockwork Canopy

1. **Choice — `wait-for-check`.** The release condition requires both sensors to be dry at the same check. The east display is blank, which is a missing reading, so the rule explicitly requires waiting. Releasing on one reading is insufficient; cancelling every future trial and replacing the west sensor are unsupported decisions.
2. **Gap — `had tested`.** The checklist says the team did not test the seal before the flight. The prompt explicitly asks for the past perfect of *test* in an unreal past if-clause; the supplied result clause completes a third conditional. The task restricts the answer to two words, so there is no alternative verb or tense.
3. **Match — A → all requirements met; B → east-ribbon reading missing; C → 09:15 signal check missing; D → clock outside the two-minute limit.** The logs vary one decisive requirement at a time, so each status has one unique match. A one-minute difference is within the limit; a four-minute difference is outside it.
4. **Order — observed south-pointing ribbon → failed wind requirement/not green → wait before public scheduling.** The prompt requires the chain “observed fact, rule result, next action,” and the rule card states the green requirements and wait condition. No other token order follows that explicit sequence.
5. **Writing — reflective only.** The example is an original recommendation based on the recorded signal and clock readings. The learner checks whether their own conditional connects a condition to an action and whether two cited facts support the recommendation. No automatic correctness or CEFR credit is implied.

## Educational limits and known gaps

This is a small content sample, not a representative assessment of either CEFR level. The CEFR describes illustrative language-use capabilities; these ten activities do not establish a learner's proficiency. The B2 decisions use a fictional, self-contained rule set, so success depends on reading and conditional reasoning rather than technical knowledge of sensors or flight equipment. The B2 linguistic demand is introductory for the band and has not been validated against a calibrated B2 measure. Multiple-choice, matching and ordering mainly test recognition and inference; they do not show whether a learner can independently use the language in conversation. The two writing activities invite self-review and are not machine-graded.

The A1 harbor clues are short and concrete, but this sample has no accompanying visual map even though visual support often helps beginning readers. Neither mission includes listening, speaking, open interaction, screen-reader review or differentiated accessibility variants. The sample covers no intermediate levels or wider topic range. These gaps should be considered before accepting or expanding the launch library.
