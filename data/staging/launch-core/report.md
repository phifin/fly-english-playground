## Answer rationale and ambiguity review

### A1 — The Shell Bell's New Song

1. **Choice — `inside-hut`.** The note places the bell inside the shell hut; it places the shells under the bench. The alternatives are distinct.
2. **Gap — `are`.** The task explicitly asks for present *be* with plural *shells*. No other present form agrees.
3. **Match — lamp → above the door; rope → beside the pier; bell → inside the hut.** The note gives a separate place for each object, so there is no competing right-side match.
4. **Order — gate, hut, bell.** “First, next, then” fixes the order. No alternative permutation is valid.
5. **Writing — self-review only.** The example follows the map; the rubric checks agreement and one accurate location clue without automatic scoring.

### A2 — The Seed Tin's Return

1. **Choice — `old-bench`.** Asha finds the tin under the bench at noon and carries it to the cart only after lunch.
2. **Gap — `found`.** The task restricts the answer to the past simple of the bracketed irregular verb *find*.
3. **Match — Moon beans → warm box; silver mint → shade; star thyme → pot beside the fountain.** The planting board gives one unique location for each seed.
4. **Order — check gate, find tin, carry tin.** The diary's morning/noon/after-lunch markers provide a single chronology.
5. **Writing — self-review only.** The example models two past events with a clear time transition; no automatic answer judgment is intended.

### A2 — The Rain Jar Schedule

1. **Choice — show the pond map.** The notice assigns this job to Lio after lunch; the walk occurs at ten.
2. **Gap — `will check`.** The prompt explicitly restricts the future form to *will* plus the base verb.
3. **Match — gate opening → nine; seed walk → ten; pond map → after lunch; garden closing → four.** Each event has one time in the notice.
4. **Order — fill jars, cover bean bed, place sign.** The notice uses first/then/last, leaving no alternative order.
5. **Writing — self-review only.** The example names future jobs and times; the rubric checks a future form and a fitting time.

### B1 — The Chart Room Letter

1. **Choice — relieved and mildly embarrassed.** Neri says she is relieved and a little foolish, so the initial suspicion was mistaken.
2. **Gap — `was copying`.** The task specifies past continuous for the action already in progress when the bell rang.
3. **Match — Neri → revises her suspicion; Jin → proposes a marker improvement; Vale → explains a misleading echo.** Each note expresses a distinct view.
4. **Order — entered room, bell rang, found chart.** The note supplies 7:30, 7:40 and later; no alternative order fits.
5. **Writing — self-review only.** The example distinguishes a first view from the clue that changed it; it is not objectively graded.

### B1 — Two Paths After Noon

1. **Choice — ridge posts stay visible.** The short pebble path's markers disappear at high tide; the ridge posts remain visible all day.
2. **Gap — although, even though, though, while or whereas.** All five can precede the full clause “the tide hides …”; the first three mark concession, while and whereas can mark contrast. Despite is excluded because this blank is followed by a subject and verb, not a noun phrase or -ing form.
3. **Match — Neri → quick at low tide; Jin → blue posts above water; guide → suitable for late arrivals.** The cited reasons are distinct.
4. **Order — pebble path before noon, ridge after noon, compass returned at the end.** Time phrases and “at the end” set one order.
5. **Writing — self-review only.** The example recommends a path using a supporting detail; scoring remains reflective.

### B2 — The Echoing Gears

1. **Choice — `two-causes`.** The report says the gear and chamber echo are both possible, with no test that separates them. The other choices overstate certainty or contradict the text.
2. **Gap — was returning.** Rowan’s quoted words use the present continuous is returning. The prompt explicitly requires standard backshift to the past continuous, so was returning is the one accepted form. A present-tense form can also be grammatical when the quoted statement remains true, but this task rules it out.
3. **Match — pulse count → recorded outcome; faded mark → possible, unproved gear issue; next-day repair → does not isolate the earlier cause.** Each item maps to a separate evidential role.
4. **Order — faint mark, three pulses, repair.** The log labels these before, during and the next morning; no alternative is valid.
5. **Writing — self-review only.** The example attributes the echo explanation to Rowan, includes the recorded pulse count and preserves the unresolved gear possibility; no automatic grade is implied.

### C1 — The Amber Line Report

1. **Choice — `suggests`.** Two scans support a small shift, but there is no independent repeat and temperature also changed. The other summaries invent certainty or contradict the note.
2. **Gap — `having compared`.** The prompt requires a perfect participle to show the comparisons were completed before the curator's action; the participle and main clause share the curator as subject.
3. **Match — moved in two scans → direct repeated observation; may have shifted → tentative inference; established → overclaim.** Each statement has a different certainty level.
4. **Order — claim, either scan observation, the other observation, limitation.** The two middle observations are independent and may switch places; `alternatives` records both accepted orders. No other order meets the required opening and closing structure.
5. **Writing — self-review only.** The example separates finding, measurement and limitation; it is not automatically scored.

### C1 — The Mirror's Earlier Signal

1. **Choice — `lower-bound`.** The second value replaces the first, while the first retains one limited use. “False” is too broad, and “confirmed exactly” reverses the text.
2. **Gap — `to have affected`.** The prompt requires a perfect infinitive after the impersonal passive and anchors the signal before the second scan. The verb is fixed by the bracket.
3. **Match — superseded by → replacement; though it remains useful → concession; is believed to have affected → attributed earlier influence without direct certainty.** The three phrases perform distinct reporting functions.
4. **Order — finding, qualification, recommendation.** The prompt names that structure explicitly; no alternative sequence is valid.
5. **Writing — self-review only.** The example corrects the absolute statement while preserving an accurate use and proposes a check.

### C2 — The Margin Marks

1. **Choice — `meaningful`.** The phrase understates a substantial influence. The context supports significance, not a claim that the marks caused every later change.
2. **Gap — `face`.** *On the face of it* is the fixed idiom for an initial appearance that later information may qualify; the blank accepts only the noun *face*.
3. **Match — tentative → may be original; qualified → limited to two copies; concessive → acknowledges small size but retains recurrence.** Each wording demonstrates one pragmatic function.
4. **Order — claim, northern-copy detail and coastal-map detail in either order, limitation.** The independent support details may switch; both valid permutations are listed. No other position follows the prompt's required claim/evidence/limit structure.
5. **Writing — self-review only.** The example treats influence as plausible without claiming a single origin. The rubric evaluates scope and certainty reflectively.

### C2 — The Quiet Caption

1. **Choice — `earlier-use`.** The correction says the chart may be accurate for its season yet less relevant now, not false in every season.
2. **Gap — `but`.** In the fixed degree expression *all but + adjective*, the phrase means “almost”; side-light shows the inscription is not entirely unreadable.
3. **Match — not necessarily original → origin remains possible but unestablished; hardly conclusive → comparison does not settle the question; nothing short of remarkable → emphatic praise.** Each phrase carries a distinct implication.
4. **Order — omission, handwritten note and compass mark in either order, limited inference.** Both clues are independent and may switch; the final sentence limits what they establish. The alternative is explicitly accepted.
5. **Writing — self-review only.** The example distinguishes the printed chart from what clues may imply and avoids saying the route was used.

Other order items use explicit time words or a named rhetorical structure and have no valid alternate order. Every gap is restricted by a form, idiom or stated structure; where a natural linker has multiple correct forms, all three are accepted. Match right sides are unique, and each choice has one answer supported by the English context.

## Educational limits and known gaps

This is a bounded original launch library, not a calibrated test. The Council of Europe descriptors are illustrative; ten activities cannot establish any learner's CEFR level. Alignment is an author judgment. The B2 item targets an unreal-past conditional, while the reading passages remain short and accessible; the B2 linguistic demand has not been validated against an external B2 measure. C1 and C2 items introduce higher-register syntax and lexical/pragmatic nuance, but their difficulty has not been calibrated with target-level learners. Cambridge entries were available in search summaries, while direct page fetches returned HTTP 403; the word meanings were not independently checked in the full entries during this turn.

Choice, gap, match and order primarily assess recognition, text interpretation and constrained language forms. They do not establish spontaneous speaking, extended writing or transfer beyond these invented settings. The writing items are reflective and receive no automatic correctness or CEFR credit. A1 has no visual harbor map, and neither pack offers audio, oral interaction or accessibility variants. Content includes no unsafe, private or sensitive themes. No independent reviewer or acceptance decision is claimed.
