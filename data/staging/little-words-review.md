# Little Words v1: staged content review

## Scope and status

This is an original, small English–Vietnamese noun pack for adult-assisted, pre-reading family play. It is a content draft awaiting independent review. The experience described for it is a flashcard followed by a three-picture listening choice, with a caregiver available to read aloud.

The research supports only the design rationale for shared, playful language: NAEYC describes guided play as a context where adults can introduce vocabulary in English and children’s home languages, while Toub et al. studied vocabulary in an adult-supported play activity following shared book reading. Those works do not validate this particular pack, its translations, its illustrations, its fit for pre-readers, or a browser game.

## Coverage and validation report

The pack contains exactly the twelve requested IDs, once each, in the requested order. Each English entry is its lowercase ID in the singular. Each cue uses the simple “Find the [word].” form and its Vietnamese counterpart is an invitation to find the pictured item. Each entry links to the two design-context source IDs defined in the JSON `sources` array.

Each parent tip suggests one visible or pretend action and a low-pressure way for a child to join through pointing, gesture, or play. The English and Vietnamese tips are each under 160 characters. Vietnamese text should receive a family or fluent-speaker localization review; this draft uses common equivalents and notes regional variation below.

## Limitations and implementation notes

- There has been no real-child or family usability study. Age suitability, learning outcomes, translation quality, accessibility, and image identification are unvalidated. The pack makes no CEFR level or assessment claim.
- This is a small selection of concrete nouns, not a complete curriculum. A caregiver should follow the child’s interest and may simply name a picture or play with the matching real object.
- Families may use regional Vietnamese variants, including “cái ly” for “cup” or “trái táo” for “apple.” Keep the visible label open to localization review rather than presenting one regional term as universal.
- Children should not need to read. A caregiver can read the English cue and name the image; optional browser speech may vary by device or be unavailable offline, so caregiver read-aloud is the fallback.
- If adapted to a three-image choice, use one clear target drawing and two visually distinct distractors. Drawings should be made originally for this project, be understandable without color alone, and avoid unnecessary background detail. Do not use timers, penalties, or unearned mastery language.
- The cited research covers different activities and samples. In particular, Toub et al.’s preschool intervention coupled shared book reading with adult-supported play; this pack does not reproduce that intervention.
