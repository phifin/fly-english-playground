## Learning and review

In **Study → Everyday English**, learners start in their selected proficiency range, see a suggested unit, and can switch ranges. Worked examples introduce the pattern before practice. The results show bilingual explanations, missed questions and earlier attempts for the selected lesson. Review prioritises missed items. Correct independent responses on separate days extend the interval. Opening help, reading a transcript or skipping playback leaves evidence marked as supported. Exam records retain their own scores and history.

Versioned IDs and fingerprints protect scored prompts, keys and stimuli in 93 course, listening and exam editions. Retain an earlier edition when publishing a changed key or materially changed question so stored answers remain interpretable. The earlier rejected expansion of 24 objectives and 72 lexical targets, 22 historical activity definitions preserved for older records, and test fixtures are excluded from the new coverage. In that rejected collection, repeated exercises and weak objective-to-task alignment made bulk promotion inappropriate.

## Highest-priority gaps

1. **Independent retrieval.** Add different unseen tasks for each core objective. Repeating one of the five questions in a written lesson supplies useful recall practice but also measures familiarity with that question.
2. **Listening.** Record and review longer multi-speaker everyday material, then build separate exam listening units with suitable pacing, accents and playback rules. This edition has 31.19 seconds of fixed human speech and no C2 listening unit.
3. **Academic breadth.** Add further competitive-school and international reading papers with fresh stimuli. The 14-question IELTS and 18-question school checkpoints have optional elapsed timers. Full-length mocks and empirically calibrated scores require substantially larger separate pools.
4. **Child and beginner depth.** Extend illustrated action verbs, spatial relationships and opportunities to follow a short spoken story. Check recognisability and task length with real children.
5. **Validation.** Use learner response patterns and independent educator review to revise provisional difficulty, distractors and sequencing. Neither completion nor a raw question score certifies a CEFR level.
