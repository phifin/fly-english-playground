# Picture vocabulary

The first 12 Little Words retain their accepted IDs and original drawings. Seven thematic batches add 56 pictured English entries, each with a Vietnamese label, an English example, its Vietnamese translation and an optional caregiver prompt. Each drawing in `assets/picture-garden.svg` was authored for Atlas. All 56 texts and pictures are originals with no external art, font or dictionary dependency. The six earlier picture-clue objects are separate from these 68 Little Words, for 74 unique pictured targets. Repeated card presentations, matching pairs, questions asking for the same object and translated labels are counted once.

Animals practise naming and imitating movement. Market items practise food names and salient features. Household items link everyday objects to functions. Clothing links names with dressing. Vehicles connect objects to modes of travel. The garden group pairs observation with gesture. Toys link object names with pretend play. A caregiver can select any theme, say a word aloud, point together or stop after one interaction. Word and sentence playback uses an available device voice. It has no recorded duration or examination listening score.

Developmental guidance informed the short rounds, optional repetition and shared play. These references informed the design and supplied no redistributed exercises or artwork. [NAEYC principles of development and learning](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/position-statements/dap/principles) and [What Works Clearinghouse review of Toub et al.](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Study/90158).

For future batches, choose concrete, visually distinguishable referents. Draw and inspect all objects together at card size, verify each spoken cue against its picture, and check that names, examples and adult invitations agree in both languages. Preserve published IDs and ordering. Run the picture-library, little-play and memory-play tests and the curriculum browser journey. The exercises record participation and supported recognition. Their suitability for an individual child and their effects on vocabulary retention require real family observation.
